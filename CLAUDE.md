## Starting a session (setup and pitfalls)
- **Repo:** `JoelsCodes/tactics-game`, branch `claude/godot-port`. To edit it, the session needs the repo attached with push access (`add_repo` with `access: push`). With read access only, a clone works but nothing can be pushed.
- **Clone:** `git clone --branch claude/godot-port https://github.com/JoelsCodes/tactics-game`. If an anonymous clone fails on Git LFS, prefix it with `GIT_LFS_SKIP_SMUDGE=1`.
- **Godot 4.3:** the binary is not kept between sessions, so install it at the start of each one (about 50 MB download, under a minute):
  ```
  mkdir -p /tmp/godot-install && cd /tmp/godot-install && \
  curl -sSL -o godot.zip https://github.com/godotengine/godot/releases/download/4.3-stable/Godot_v4.3-stable_linux.x86_64.zip && \
  unzip -q -o godot.zip && install -m 755 Godot_v4.3-stable_linux.x86_64 /usr/local/bin/godot && godot --version
  ```
  Expected output: `4.3.stable.official.77dcf97d8`. Downloading a binary can be blocked by the session's permission check; if it is, ask the owner rather than working around it.
- **Screenshots and the 3D board:** `apt-get update && apt-get install -y mesa-vulkan-drivers xvfb`. Headless tests need neither. Python needs Pillow, numpy and scipy for the art tools.
- **First run in a fresh clone:** `godot --headless --path godot --import` before any screenshot or test. Without it a screenshot can show old art from the import cache.
- **Commits:** the owner dropped the old "push only when I say push" rule (Nov 2026): commit and push finished work to `claude/godot-port`, and say what changed.
- **Art rebuilds:** `bash godot/tools/art/build_characters.sh` rewrites every `assets/characters/*.tres`. Afterwards, restore the characters you did not change with `git checkout -- assets/characters/<name>.tres` so the diff stays small.
- **Attacks and hits:** the King, the Ashen Squire, Vesper, Lunar and the Sorceress have four-direction attacks from `art_src/characters/{king,squire,vesper,wraith,sorc}_attack_sheet.png`, cut by `tools/art/attack_sheet.py` through `king_attack.py`, `squire_attack.py`, `vesper_attack.py`, `wraith_attack.py` and `sorc_attack.py`. Their `meta.json` entries set `slash_in_frames`, so battles add no second slash. A hit flashes the unit white and knocks it back a little (`UnitView3D.hit_react`); the hurt pose is not played on a hit (its frames are kept in the sheets). Hit frames for review: `tests/hit_shot.tscn`.
- **Outside castle (unused):** `scripts/stage/outside_castle.gd` and `outside_blur.gd` can build a blurred castle around a map (`Cutscene3D.setup_scene(map, true)`). The owner replaced it with the normal gradient backdrop (Nov 2026): no scene uses it now.

## Where the game stands
- Story (reworked Nov 2026): intro (forest chase, Alaric hurt) → hallway (squire catches him, slash) → first battle (Alaric, Rook style, one squire) → `hallway_return` (boots, he limps on) → `chamber` (altar, Bram and Lyra, the owner's dialogue) → chamber battle (two squires, summon tutorial) → `base` (the base conversation, which opens the riverbank) → the **world map**. From there: the base (a free-roam 30x30 area; its altar opens the barracks, its gate the world map) and the maps of each open place, replayable. The King unit is called **Alaric**. The narration, the base dialogue, the tutorial texts and Bram's and Lyra's numbers are Claude's first drafts: see `godot/HANDOFF.md`.
- **Chapter two, Riverford (Nov 2026, "The Price of Embers"):** five story battles (`Riverford`, `samples/riverford_1..5.json`, made by `tools/make_riverford_maps.py`) with six story scenes before and after them (`RiverfordStory`, played by `StoryScene`; the first pick of a map plays its scene and then the battle; each scene plays once per save, `CampaignState.scenes_seen`; a replay never plays one). Merc joins as a vessel in scene `rf_awakening`. The owner's text is used word for word; Claude's drafted lines are marked DRAFT in `riverford_story.gd` and listed in `godot/HANDOFF.md`. Enemies are rank E skill users only (`Riverford.KINDS`). Map 5 has waves and Vesper flees (`BattleSetup.waves`, `flee`). See `godot/README.md`.
- **Chapter three, the Crucible (Nov 2026, "The Crucible and the Crown"):** `Crucible` (`samples/crucible_1..3.json`, `tools/make_crucible_maps.py`): three BIG maps (16 deep, 32 to 40 wide, the battle camera scrolls) on a new tileset (marble, snow, sand, isle stone, lava), new enemies with rank E and occasional rank D skills (`Crucible.KINDS`, summon stones carry a rank D skill about 30% of the time), scenes in `CrucibleStory` (`cr_table` chains into `cr_gate`, then `cr_pincer`, `cr_breaking`, `cr_end`). In `cr_breaking` Lyra becomes a Co-Captain (`Crucible.unlock_party`) and Gideon (Ashen Squire sheet) and Kaelen (Hollow Choir sheet) join as vessels. **Lyra is a Co-Captain or a vessel, never both** (`PartyRoster.stand_down`, `CampaignFlow.prepare_deployment`, the barracks button). Unlocked by clearing Riverford's last map (`WorldData.UNLOCKED_BY_CLEARING`).
- **Status ailments and the enemy AI (Nov 2026):** sleep, stun, confuse, berserk, doom, curse, blind, slowed, haste (`BattleUnit`, skills in `data/skills`, `tests/ailments_check.tscn`); `EnemyAI` weighs danger, focus, ground, company, roles (`EnemyData.ai_role`) and likes to use every skill it has (`EnemyAI.WEIGHTS`).
- **Difficulty and balance (Nov 2026):** `bash godot/tools/balance/run.sh runs=12` simulates every story battle with the AI on both sides and prints win rates and who does the damage (`tests/balance_sim.gd`; tuning in `Riverford.TUNE` / `Crucible.TUNE`). Read `godot/HANDOFF.md` ("The balance simulator") before trusting the numbers: the AI party plays worse than a person.
- **Skills (Nov 2026):** 150+ skills in all six proficiencies, ranks E to S (`data/skills/*.json`, generated from `tools/skills/build_skills.py`, effects in `SkillFx`). Only the owner's rank E physical and healing skills are the plain-enemy default (`SkillLibrary.enemy_pool`).
- Open questions for the owner: what comes after the base conversation; what `samples/abandoned_hallway.json` is for; whether the castle and blur around the hallway stay; Lyra is a vessel now (the sample party's first Co-Captain is a placeholder Sorceress).
- Known limits: the squire's and King's shared canvases clip the far tips of their widest arcs (the King's down attack most); story and battle pushes still move a unit to its new tile.

## Design rules the owner has set (do not undo)
- Loadout slots are exactly: Main Weapon, Weapon Style, Relic Slot 1, Relic Slot 2, plus the vessels-only Temp-Relic. Everything is set in the out-of-battle menus; nothing changes in battle.
- No pre-battle screen. The King is fixed on every mission; the player picks the Co-Captain in the barracks.
- **Weapon styles and stances (owner, Nov 2026; the table is the `StanceRules` header, `docs` in the README):** a Physical skill's footprint comes only from the style and stance, skills have no shape of their own. Every style has Impact and Pierce; only the Pawn also has Axe. Rook and Bishop and Pawn face forward (placed facing, then the direction of the last step; attacking never turns a unit). Rook/Bishop/Queen hit along a line the whole map (Queen Impact only 2 tiles), 33% for 1-3 tiles then 1/D, Impact stops at the first unit, Pierce hits all; Knight jumps an L; King hits the 8 around; Pawn hits the front. Higher ground than the attacker blocks, every attack hits ally and foe, a skill's own full damage replaces the 100%. `tests/board_check` (`stances`) checks every style.
- **Enemy styles and sword drops (owner, Nov 2026):** every enemy rolls a random style (never King or Queen) and allowed stance each battle, except the tutorial enemies (`BattleSetup.fixed_styles`). A fallen enemy leaves a slightly transparent sword with its style and stance; only a player-side unit that ENDS ITS TURN on it takes it (walking over it does not; it rises and vanishes) and only that unit can equip that style and stance from the loadout menu from then on (`UnitData.known_styles`, saved). The sword follows the tile rule (1.1 units tall, well inside one tile). `tests/sword_check.tscn`.
- Battle spacing (owner, Nov 2026): on the first turn every opposing pair is farther apart than Move plus 1 tile; weapon reach does not count (`tests/spacing_check.gd`).
- Every map's outer rim is a cutaway cross-section about 2.5 tiles deep (`Board3D._cutaway`, `BASE`, `CUT_BANDS`): soil, masonry or bedrock by the tile's side texture, darker with depth. New maps get it for free.
- **Diorama boundary standard (owner, Nov 2026, approved after review): every map's floating-island edge is built the same way, with no per-map setup.** The cutaway rim is a lumpy, tapering cliff (`Board3D._rim_wall`, `_rim_warp`) shaded by `shaders/diorama_edge.gdshader` (strata, crevices, moss, sinking into the void and dissolving into the mist at the bottom); two mist sheets wrap its foot (`RimMist0/1`, `shaders/rim_mist.gdshader`); roots, ivy, boulders, rock spikes and a grass or rubble lip hang over it (`Board3D._build_overhangs`). New maps get it for free from `Board3D.setup`. Do not replace it with a flat slice, remove the mist or the dissolve, or give a map its own edge; tune the constants (`RIM_TAPER`, shader uniforms) for a map's look instead, and ask before changing the look. `tests/diorama_check.tscn` checks every sample map has it.
- **Battle direction (owner, Nov 2026): the fight runs LEFT TO RIGHT on screen, not front to back.** The player starts on the left or the right and the enemy on the opposite side; the long axis is the screen's horizontal (y), the depth (x, up the screen) stays 16 or less. The Riverford maps start Alaric on the left in maps 1, 2, 3 and 5 and on the right in map 4 (`tools/make_riverford_maps.py` builds each in its final orientation). New maps follow it; `tests/riverford_check.gd` checks the sides.
- Every battle map starts with nobody able to reach anybody on the first turn: each opposing pair is farther apart than Move plus 1 tile (`tests/spacing_check.gd`; weapon reach no longer counts). Place units and size maps to keep it true. **Enemies do not have to start across the map (far x): from now on they may start to the left or right of the player, or in a mix of sides, as long as it takes them more than one turn to get to the player.** The check is direction-agnostic; the Riverford maps mostly put the enemy across, but map 2 has foes on three sides and map 5 brings reinforcements from the east.
- **Four directions only ("4d", owner, Nov 2026): every sprite, new sheet and animation is down, right, up and left. No diagonal or eight-direction art.** The data has only `<anim>_s`, `_e`, `_n` and `_w` (the old `_se`/`_sw`/`_ne`/`_nw` names and their frames were removed in Nov 2026; `meta.json`'s `eight_dir` flag only means "has directional frames" and keeps its old name).
- Lyra is a vessel (with Bram), not a Co-Captain. **Vesper is an enemy** (like Robin and the Red Wolf: `UnitLook.ENEMY_CHARACTERS`), not a Co-Captain. **The barracks shows no Co-Captain rows, buttons or wording until the player has unlocked a Co-Captain** (the story's party has none yet; the sample party with its two placeholder Co-Captains is only for tests and the sample fight).
- **Battle map size (owner, Nov 2026): the standard map is 16x16 unless told otherwise.** The depth (x, up the screen) stays 16 (never more than 19): a deeper board runs under the battle HUD. **The width can grow much further: a board up to about 24 wide still fits the screen at the fixed camera, and wider ones (up to 40) make the battle camera scroll along them** (`BattleStage.scrolling`, `Board3D.scroll_range` / `scroll_to`): same lens, angle and distance, so sprites keep their size; it follows whoever acts, the walk, the aimed tile, and the arrow keys / A and D / the mouse at the screen's sides move it; it never goes past the board's edges. Riverford maps are 16 deep and 20 to 24 wide (the old 15x15 Riverbank maps are gone). (The base is a free-roam area, not a battle map: it is 30x30 at the owner's request.) `tests/riverford_check.gd` checks the cap.
- Battle maps have routes that block units (deep water, cliffs two levels high, trees, walls) so the road to the enemy is a detour, not a straight line, and several levels of height (`tools/make_riverford_maps.py` measures the detour when it builds a map).
- Every summoning stone (the glowing field relic a vessel is summoned onto) carries one random rank E physical skill (`SummonStone.make`, from `SkillLibrary`), which the vessel uses and starts learning.
- Terrain negative: a unit that ends its turn in shallow water loses 1 Move on its next turn only (`BattleController.WADING_SLOW`, using `BattleUnit.slow`).
- The world map (Phantom Brave style): places open one at a time as the story unlocks them; a place has maps, a map has levels. **Every map is locked until the player has done its story mission once. A replay unlocks nothing.** A replay is the same battle with no story beats or tutorial, played with the player's real party (every unit at its own level, every vessel): the point of replaying is to get stronger, so the enemies give everything they normally give (XP, relics) and it is all kept and saved; a replay never changes or takes away anything the player has. The base is a place on it (30x30), and it is walked in freely (not tile by tile).
- Vessels are not pre-selected: the King summons any vessel he owns during battle, up to the map's summon limit (default 6, every summon counts).

Test headless, check layout changes with a 1920x1080 screenshot, and say that it was not checked on a real display. The game targets a 16:9 desktop screen, not phones.
- The camera keeps one lens, angle and distance everywhere (`Board3D.CAMERA_FOV`, `CAMERA_DISTANCE`) and never zooms or turns (no Rotate view). It is static on a map that fits the screen. **Two exceptions, chosen by the owner (Nov 2026): the base (30x30, `BaseArea`, `Board3D.follow`) and any battle map wider than the screen (`BattleStage` scrolling, `Board3D.scroll_to`) pan along the map with the same lens, angle and distance, stopping at the map's edges.** Unit sprites are turned back upright on screen wherever they stand (`Board3D.upright_orient`, capped at 15 degrees), re-applied on every camera move. Story scenes and battles share one framing (`BattleStage.lock_camera`), so a scene turning into a battle does not shift. Maps must fit in that view.
- Every character is drawn as tall as the King (the owner's size standard, `idle_walk_sheet.STANDARD_FIGURE`); see the skill below. **Tile rule (owner, Nov 2026): every unit must fit inside one tile, and none may be taller than the King.** A tile is 90.5 px in sprite units (frame px x `meta.json` scale; `idle_walk_sheet.TILE_WIDTH` 89.5 is the cap). A wide creature (the Eater slime, the Boar) is scaled down until its idle and walk frames fit one tile, even if that makes it much shorter than the King (Eater scale 1.45, Boar 1.0407); attack frames may reach past the tile for a moment. New characters: use `grid_sheet`/`standard_scale(..., tile_fit=True)` or check width by hand, and never tune by eye. `anim_check` `tile_fit` checks the Eater and the Boar. Lunar (wraith), the Red Wolf and the Sorceress pre-date the rule and are wider than a tile in idle: do not change them without the owner's say.
- New character sheets from the owner: follow `.claude/skills/add-character-sheet/SKILL.md`; `bash godot/tools/art/build_characters.sh` rebuilds all characters. Do not spend time tuning the colour-look recolors.
- Never use `pkill -f`.
