## The balance simulator (Nov 2026, the owner asked to be able to test difficulty)
`bash godot/tools/balance/run.sh [runs=20] [maps=t,r1..r5,c1..c3] [party=0] [hp=1.0] [atk=0] [mag=0] [drop=0] [nerve=0.25] [kingnerve=1.6] [seed=1000]` runs `tests/balance_sim.tscn` once per map in parallel (about 3 minutes for all nine maps at 12 runs) and prints one table: win rate (with its margin), verdict (easy over 90%, fair 60 to 90, hard 30 to 60, brutal under 30), rounds, party HP left in wins, party KOs, King KO rate, stalled fights, and under it who deals the damage (per enemy kind, per party unit, summons used, friendly fire). It plays the REAL maps, stats and rules with `EnemyAI` on both sides: the party summons its vessels as the fight gets close, waves arrive on their rounds, Vesper flees. The knobs try a change before editing the data (`hp`, `atk`, `mag` scale or shift every enemy, `drop` leaves enemies out, `party` shifts the party's levels). `t` is the owner's two-squire tutorial fight as a yardstick. Per-map tuning lives in `Riverford.TUNE` and `Crucible.TUNE` (hp multiplier, atk and mag shift, enemies dropped from the first group and from each wave); change those and run again. Output also goes to `/tmp/balance/<map>.json`.
**What it found and fixed:** a real game bug (the battle never ended when the King was down and the last vessel's Fade Clock ran out: `BattleController.end_turn` now checks the outcome; `ailments_check` covers it) and AI stalemates (units dancing round each other because the Rook, Bishop and Pawn only hit the way they face: a trail memory and a "step away to turn round" rule in `EnemyAI`, and a hurt unit that only ran away: where a unit stands can now only tip a close call, never talk it out of a good attack).
**What it says now (16 runs each, AI party):** tutorial 94%, r1 38%, r2 63%, r3 69%, r4 50%, **r5 0%, c1 0%, c2 0%, c3 6%**. Read it as a ramp for an AI party that plays worse than a person (no plan for terrain, no patience): chapter two's first four maps are fair to hard. **r5 and the first two Crucible maps stay brutal even with the enemies at half HP**: the party only does about 50 to 60 damage a fight in the simulation (the Rook, Bishop and Queen styles hit for 33% at close range, the vessels fade after 6 turns, and the King, whom the enemies focus, falls in nearly every fight, which cuts every vessel loose). That points at the **party's power curve, not the enemy numbers** (the owner's rules): if the owner finds those maps too hard in play, the levers are the party (more damage, more time for vessels, a way to shelter the King) before the enemies. I did not gut the enemies further on the simulator's word alone.
**Not done:** a smarter simulated player (it summons by a rule, never uses terrain on purpose), levels that grow with XP, an item or relic economy.
