## The balance simulator (Nov 2026, the owner asked to be able to test difficulty)
`bash godot/tools/balance/run.sh [runs=20] [maps=t,r1..r5,c1..c3] [party=0] [hp=1.0] [atk=0] [mag=0] [drop=0] [nerve=0.25] [kingnerve=1.6] [seed=1000]` runs `tests/balance_sim.tscn` once per map in parallel (about 3 minutes for all nine maps at 12 runs) and prints one table: win rate (with its margin), verdict (easy over 90%, fair 60 to 90, hard 30 to 60, brutal under 30), rounds, party HP left in wins, party KOs, King KO rate, stalled fights, and under it who deals the damage (per enemy kind, per party unit, summons used, friendly fire). It plays the REAL maps, stats and rules with `EnemyAI` on both sides: the party summons its vessels as the fight gets close, waves arrive on their rounds, Vesper flees. The knobs try a change before editing the data (`hp`, `atk`, `mag` scale or shift every enemy, `drop` leaves enemies out, `party` shifts the party's levels). `t` is the owner's two-squire tutorial fight as a yardstick. Per-map tuning lives in `Riverford.TUNE` and `Crucible.TUNE` (hp multiplier, atk and mag shift, enemies dropped from the first group and from each wave); change those and run again. Output also goes to `/tmp/balance/<map>.json`.
**What it found and fixed:** a real game bug (the battle never ended when the King was down and the last vessel's Fade Clock ran out: `BattleController.end_turn` now checks the outcome; `ailments_check` covers it) and AI stalemates (units dancing round each other because the Rook, Bishop and Pawn only hit the way they face: a trail memory and a "step away to turn round" rule in `EnemyAI`, and a hurt unit that only ran away: where a unit stands can now only tip a close call, never talk it out of a good attack).
**What it says now (16 runs each, AI party):** tutorial 94%, r1 38%, r2 63%, r3 69%, r4 50%, **r5 0%, c1 0%, c2 0%, c3 6%**. Read it as a ramp for an AI party that plays worse than a person (no plan for terrain, no patience): chapter two's first four maps are fair to hard. **r5 and the first two Crucible maps stay brutal even with the enemies at half HP**: the party only does about 50 to 60 damage a fight in the simulation (the Rook, Bishop and Queen styles hit for 33% at close range, the vessels fade after 6 turns, and the King, whom the enemies focus, falls in nearly every fight, which cuts every vessel loose). That points at the **party's power curve, not the enemy numbers** (the owner's rules): if the owner finds those maps too hard in play, the levers are the party (more damage, more time for vessels, a way to shelter the King) before the enemies. I did not gut the enemies further on the simulator's word alone.
**Not done:** a smarter simulated player (it summons by a rule, never uses terrain on purpose), levels that grow with XP, an item or relic economy.

## Weathered walls (Nov 2026, the owner: walls should look like terrain that happened)
Maps with `"rough_walls": true` (all ten chapter three and four maps; written by `life_kit.weather`) build their "q" brick and "r" crag tiles three or more levels high as weathered stone in `Board3D` (`_is_rough`, `_rough_pt`, `_rough_wall`, `_rock_col`): corners wander by position-based noise (so neighbouring tiles still meet), faces are split into facets that bulge and cave in, vertex colours add moss at the foot and streaks higher up, the side texture is picked per tile so it does not repeat in a lattice, and the top corners are uneven. In the map data (`weather` in `tools/life_kit.py`): natural cliffs (`q` tiles at the near and far edges) become crag rock `r`, ruin walls crumble at their ends (4 -> 3) and rise in places (+1), cliff skylines are ragged, the near cliff stays at 3 so it never hides the fight, and loose rubble lies at the foot of walls. Walls that block attacks stay at 4 or more; `q` and `r` were never impassable in the grid (only height decides), so the fighting is unchanged. Not applied to Riverford, the base or the castle maps (no flag set); low slabs and pavements stay crisp. Seen in still frames only, not on a real display.

## Battle smoothness (Nov 13 2026, the owner: "our battle still feels not smooth for some reason")
Measured with headless scripts (the AI, CPU only: real numbers) and a software-rendered profile (`tests/perf_probe.tscn`, relative numbers only). Nothing was timed on a real GPU.
- **Found and fixed: the enemy AI froze the game at the start of every enemy turn** (50 to 700 ms by map; Crown 4 and Crucible 3 worst). `EnemyAI.plan` tries every reachable tile x up to four facings x every action x every aim, each through `BattleController.preview`; the time went to (a) `StyleResolver.affected` re-deriving the aimable tiles for every aim, (b) string tests (`flags(c).contains(...)`, terrain in lists) on every grid query, (c) the same area recomputed for each of a unit's seven weapon skills, (d) `BattleUnit.stat` recomputing equipment sums on every damage call. Fixes: `TacticalGrid._bits`; `known_aimable` / `known_area` parameters; aims with nobody in their area skipped; `BattleUnit.begin_planning` (stats remembered for one plan). Result: about 3x faster overall, identical plans. `EnemyAI.fast` (default true) turns the shortcuts off so `tests/ai_speed_check` can compare. **Then time-sliced (same day):** the slowest plans were still 100 to 300 ms in one go on the 16x28 to 16x40 maps, so the stage calls `EnemyAI.plan_async` (`_plan_start` / `_search_cell` / `_plan_end`, yielding a frame every `SLICE_MS`). It only yields between two tiles it tries, when the unit and the grid are back where they were, and nothing else changes the battle during an enemy turn; a closed battle ends it. A worker thread was rejected: the search moves the unit on the real grid. `tests/stage_check` no longer expects a wide map's camera to stand still while enemies act (`same_view`).
- **Found and fixed: walks stuttered once per tile** (one awaited tween per tile left the unit still for a frame between tiles). `BattleStage.walk` is one chained tween.
- **Found and fixed: texture readbacks.** `UnitView3D._white` read each frame's texture back from the GPU to build a white silhouette (used by the hit flash, and, since Nov 12, by the ghost and the afterimages every frame). Gone: `shaders/unit_flat.gdshader` draws the flash and the afterimages, `unit_ghost.gdshader` the ghost, all from the frame's own texture. `tests/hit_check` now checks the shader instead of the image.
- **Added: graphics quality (F8)**, `Board3D.apply_quality`. Software-render profile, idle battle, 1280x720, ms per frame (relative): High 416 ms, Medium 397 ms, Low 174 ms (software rendering is fill-rate bound, so it understates what dropping the fill-light shadows saves on a GPU: Medium cuts the geometry from 318,000 to 92,000 triangles a frame; Low is 2.4x faster by dropping the full-screen effects). Why the fill lights: each shadowed omni light redraws the whole board six times; with them the board is 1,234 draw calls and 318,000 triangles a frame, without them 936 and 92,000.
- **Not done (ideas, in order of expected gain):** pre-warm effect shaders and the first use of particle and `FxSprite` materials at battle start (Godot 4.3 compiles a shader the first time it is seen, which hitches the first spell of a battle); fewer render objects (about 100 mesh instances are tile tops and walls that could be merged per height and terrain); lower the shadow map size or the soft-shadow quality; render at a lower 3D scale on weak machines.
